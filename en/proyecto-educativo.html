Institutional educational project

How we learn

Our educational project combines the standards of the Colombian Ministry of Education with active-learning practice. We cover the national curriculum; what changes is the road we take to get there.

The approach

What is active learning?

It is a way of understanding education in which the student leads their own process and the teacher is a well-prepared companion. It is not about letting go: it is about preparing the environment, observing closely and stepping in at the right moment.

In practice, that means a maths lesson can begin with a real problem, that writing grows out of a concrete experience, and that a conflict at break time becomes material for learning how to live together.

  • Project-based learning built around each group's own questions.
  • Prepared environments with materials suited to each age.
  • Movement and play as needs, not as rewards.
  • Teachers trained in observation, support and subject didactics.

The pillars

Six dimensions we work on at once

None replaces another. A well-educated person needs all six.

Cognitive

Language, mathematical thinking, natural and social sciences, technology and English, with the national competency standards as the required reference.

Social and emotional

Recognising what you feel, naming it and resolving conflicts with words. Regular dialogue circles in every grade.

Physical

Motor skills, sport and physical expression. The body as the first instrument of knowledge.

Artistic

Visual arts, music and drama, with a showcase open to the community. Art as a language, not as a filler subject.

Civic and environmental

The school environmental project, student government and community service projects. Citizenship is learned by practising it.

Bilingual programme

English from preschool with a communicative approach, reinforced in secondary school with national exams and higher education in mind.

Environments

The whole campus is the classroom

Every space has a defined pedagogical purpose and someone responsible for it.

Laboratories

Natural sciences, physics and chemistry with hands-on experimental work from lower secondary onwards.

Library

A collection organised by level, reading rooms and a whole-school reading plan connected to every subject.

Art studios

Visual arts, music and drama, with a showcase open to the community every semester.

Classrooms

Spaces with learning corners, a classroom library and collaborative work tables instead of rows.

Sports area

Courts and free-play areas. Developmental sport, with an emphasis on cooperative play.

The main courtyard

Assemblies, dialogue circles, celebrations and showcases open to families.

Assessment

We assess processes, not just exams

Our institutional assessment system complies with Decree 1290 of 2009 and translates our way of supporting students into criteria that families can understand.

  • Continuous observation recorded by the class teacher.
  • Portfolio with evidence of each student's real work.
  • Self- and peer assessment from primary school onwards.
  • Descriptive reports each term, beyond the numerical grade.
  • Individual meetings with every family at the end of each term.
