Programs

From pre-kindergarten to grade 11, on one campus

Fourteen grades and a single pedagogical project. Students who join at age three can graduate in the same place where they learned to read, accompanied by a team that has known them all along.

Calendar A Full day and mornings Co-educational Max. 25 per group

Ages 3 to 5 · Pre-kindergarten, kindergarten and transition

Preschool

Early childhood should not be rushed. Over these three years the core work is secure attachment, play, movement and language development. Reading, writing and number appear when the child is ready, not when the calendar says so.

We work with prepared environments organised into corners, hands-on materials, stable routines and plenty of time outdoors. The transition into first grade is gradual and supported.

  • Free play and guided play with pedagogical intent.
  • Fine and gross motor skills, and sensory development.
  • A natural approach to reading, writing and number.
  • Playful English, music and physical expression.
  • Support with settling in and with everyday independence.

Ages 6 to 10 · Grades 1 to 5

Primary school

This is the stage of the big tools: reading with comprehension, writing in your own voice and thinking mathematically. Work is organised into projects that bring several subjects together around a real question from the group.

The whole-school reading plan and the use of concrete materials in mathematics are the two axes of these five years.

  • Spanish language with a classroom library and creative writing.
  • Mathematics with concrete materials before formal algorithms.
  • Natural and social sciences through research projects.
  • English, visual arts, music and physical education.
  • Weekly dialogue circles in every grade.

Ages 11 to 14 · Grades 6 to 9

Lower secondary

Adolescence calls for real challenges and adults who hold steady. Students go deeper into each discipline with specialist teachers, without losing project work or the relationship with their form tutor.

This is where the laboratory, methodical research, reasoned debate and the first community responsibilities within the school appear.

  • Core subjects taught by specialist teachers.
  • Science laboratory and school research projects.
  • Technology and computing, with responsible use of digital tools.
  • Art, drama and music with a public showcase each semester.
  • Student government and participation in the student council.

Ages 15 to 17 · Grades 10 and 11

Upper secondary

The final two years combine the academic demands of higher education with the career guidance that life demands. Focused preparation for the national Saber 11 exams, without turning the year into one long mock test.

Every student develops their own final project and completes the compulsory community service in local organisations.

  • Saber 11 preparation with mock exams and subject support.
  • Final project with an assigned supervisor.
  • Compulsory community service (80 hours).
  • Career guidance and support with university applications.
  • Advanced English for standardised tests.

A day at school

How the day is organised

Timetables vary by level, but the shape of the day is shared: gathering, deep work, movement, food and closing.

Structure of a typical school day
TimeMomentWhat happens
7:00 – 7:30Arrival and circleWelcome, group assembly and agreements for the day.
7:30 – 9:30Deep workA long, uninterrupted block of project or core subject work.
9:30 – 10:15Break and snackFree play and movement outdoors.
10:15 – 12:00Subjects and workshopsEnglish, arts, science, sport or laboratory, depending on the day.
12:00 – 1:00LunchDining hall, with students taking turns to serve.
1:00 – 3:00Project afternoonProject work, support sessions and individual attention.
3:00 – 3:30ClosingTidying up, group closing circle and school transport departure.

Indicative timetable. The preschool day and the morning schedule are shorter.
